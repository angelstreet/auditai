# Auditor guide: run an audit from start to finish

For the owner and auditors. Plain steps in the app, in the order you do them. The why behind each rule is in
[AUDIT_WORKFLOW.md](AUDIT_WORKFLOW.md); commands that need a terminal are in [RUNBOOK.md](RUNBOOK.md).

The app's tabs: **Portfolio, Engagements, Invitations, Teams, Synergies, Reports, Data, Names to hide, Audit
trail**. The organisation you work on is chosen in the header and stays chosen.

Accounts need only an email address: no name is asked or shown anywhere; people are labelled by their address and role.

## Before the survey

1. **Agree the basics with the client** (outside the app): who is controller, retention, the approved AI route,
   who the referent is. Nothing real goes in before the processing agreement is signed.
2. **Engagements**: create the engagement (name, language, dates), then add the teams and their size. The
   engagement settings page holds the privacy notice: fill it before the first link goes out.
3. **Names to hide**: add the client's company name, product names and the names of people who might be mentioned
   in answers. They are hidden before any model sees the text and refused in the published report.
4. **Teams, Referents**: give the referent a login (single-use link to set a password). The referent sees counts
   and approved results only, never who answered.
5. **Invitations**: set the closing date and the contact person, then create the links (or let the referent do it
   from their space). Launch the engagement when ready.

## While people answer

6. Follow progress on **Invitations** (per team, groups under 5 people are not shown as exact numbers). The
   referent sends one generic reminder; nobody is chased by name.

## Review

7. **Engagements, Analyses**: read each analysis (themes, contradictions, interview questions, with the source of
   every claim). Approve what is right, leave the rest. An "AI draft" stays a draft until you approve it.
8. **Engagements, Opportunities**: turn approved analyses into opportunities, set an impact score if you know it,
   and move the good ones to Approved. Only approved opportunities reach the report.

## Build the audit report

9. **Reports**: press "Start the report" on the audit. The app proposes up to three improvement steps for each
   approved opportunity, taken from a catalogue (simplify, automate, train, AI assistant, data and tools, rules),
   with a line saying why each was proposed.
10. For each step: **Accept**, **Reject**, or the pencil to **Edit** effort, impact, risk, the owner (a role,
    such as "Team lead", never a person's name) and when (30, 60 or 90 days). "From the catalogue" adds another
    catalogue step; "Write my own" adds yours. "Suggest again" covers opportunities approved later.
11. **Executive summary**: "Write a first draft" fills it from the score, the most raised findings and the first
    steps. Rewrite it in your own words for the sponsor. Tick the findings it rests on: every claim keeps its
    source. No names, no quotes from answers. If the owner ran the AI draft, it shows as "AI draft, not reviewed";
    "Use this draft" copies it in, and you still edit it.
12. **Readiness score**: nothing to do. It is computed from the survey answers per axis (knowledge, use,
    conditions), each counting for a third, shown with what the level means, and per team only when at least 5
    people answered.
13. **Next 30, 60 and 90 days**: "Build from the accepted steps", then edit, add or remove lines. Each horizon ends
    with a checkpoint (check use, measure early results, keep, adapt or stop).
14. Fill **Risks** (one per line) and check the **Method and privacy** note.

## Check and publish

15. "Mark ready for review", then **Preview as the client**. You see exactly what the client will see, or the
    reason it is not ready (for example: no finding ticked, a name from the list, words copied from an answer).
16. **Publish** and confirm. The referent now sees it in the **Report** tab of their space and can download the
    PDF. Download it yourself from the editor to send it to the sponsor.
17. To change a published report, "Start a new version": the client keeps the published version until you
    publish the new one. "Unpublish" removes it from the client at once.

Every step above is written to the **Audit trail** (who, when, what), without the report text.

## After the report

18. Follow the 30/60/90-day checkpoints with the sponsor. Pilot tracking and measured benefits in the app are a
    later phase; until then, keep the measures with the client and start a new report version if the plan changes.
19. Retention and erasure requests: **Data** page.
